\section{Supplementary methods}

\subsection{Interpretation of the stochastic-accessibility estimand}
The origin--destination Markov chain represents aggregate survey-weighted population
flows. A first-passage step is a dimensionless transition in this fitted flow system and
must not be interpreted as a minute of travel or as an observed individual's itinerary.
For every purpose, residential distributions are conditioned on starting outside the
corresponding target set in the primary specification.

\subsection{Regularization sensitivity}
Group-specific rows are shrunk toward the population kernel using
$\omega_{ig}=\mathrm{ESS}_{ig}/(\mathrm{ESS}_{ig}+\tau)$. The primary analysis uses
$\tau=20$. We re-estimated both territorial systems at $\tau\in\{0,10,20,50\}$.
At $\tau=0$, origins with positive group-specific support use the empirical row, whereas
origins without such support retain the population row so that the transition kernel
remains defined. Table~\ref{tab:s1_tau} reports the total gap, its paired scope contrast,
the corresponding residential and kernel contrasts, and the normalized-gap contrast.
It also retains the origin-level magnitude and rank diagnostics relative to $\tau=20$.
These are point-estimate sensitivity calculations; inferential intervals in the main
analysis correspond to the primary $\tau=20$ specification.

\subsection{Specification grid}
The effective robustness grid crosses two target definitions (core 50\% and extended
80\% of supported weighted arrivals) and two residential conventions (outside-target
conditioning or all residences) across three purposes, yielding 12 specifications.
An initial audit also reconstructed the population reference using only observations
with valid sex categories. Valid-category coverage was nearly complete, and estimates
differed from the full-population reference only below the reported precision. We
therefore retain the full-population reference and do not count the practically duplicate
rows as additional specifications. These checks assess specification dependence; they
were not used to select the primary result.

\subsection{Multiscale territorial construction}
External municipalities are ranked by total bidirectional survey-weighted flow with
Bogotá using the complete population. Nested functional scopes include the minimum
ranked municipalities required to reach at least 50\% and 80\% of that interface flow.
Together with Bogotá D.C. and the complete regional network, the realized interface
shares are 0, 0.564, 0.824, and 1. Within every scope, the largest directed strongly
connected component, population kernel, purpose targets, group kernels, and residential
distributions are reconstructed. The full-region endpoint retains all 141 nodes in the
validated regional kernel, including one node without a municipality label.

\subsection{Bootstrap inference}
The final regional analysis uses 500 joint Poisson(1) household-cluster bootstrap
replicates. The same multiplier is assigned to every record from a household and is
propagated jointly to person and trip weights. Simultaneous intervals use a bootstrap
max-$t$ critical value over the three destination purposes within each effect family.
The paired scope analysis likewise uses 500 shared household multipliers in the regional
and district systems. The earlier 50-replicate specification grid and 200-replicate
standalone district analysis are robustness diagnostics rather than the final paired
inference.

\subsection{Paired specification-grid sensitivity}
The regional-minus-district contrast was recalculated for the same 12 effective
target-definition and conditioning combinations. All total-gap, kernel-component, and
relative-gap contrasts remained positive. Magnitudes were nevertheless
specification-dependent: the smallest employment and education contrasts occurred when
the extended 80\% target was combined with all residences. These are point-estimate

\subsection{Full municipal omission and sample support}
As a sensitivity diagnostic distinct from fixed-kernel origin attribution, each of the
20 external municipalities is omitted in turn. We remove its UTAMs, all incident
survey trips, and its residents; then reconstruct the largest directed strongly
connected component, population kernel, sex-specific kernels, residential
distributions, and purpose-specific core-50 targets. The Bogotá D.C. reference
remains fixed. A regional UTAM without a municipality label is retained in
all fits. All 20 refits and the unmodified 141-node regional baseline pass the
reconstruction checks. Facatativá and Soacha have respectively 229 and 1,449
sampled outbound households; their female/male outbound trip effective sample
sizes are 299/241 and 2,246/1,944. These support statistics describe the
available sample and do not quantify uncertainty in the refitted contrasts.
Table~\ref{tab:s11_municipality_refit} gives the ranges and the two most
relevant named examples. This diagnostic redefines the network and target
geography and is neither a paired bootstrap test nor a causal deletion effect.

\subsection{One-run reviewer validation: time-access benchmark and home-origin kernels}
A fixed-time comparator was built on the same directed UTAM networks and
scope-specific core-50 purpose sets as the primary analysis. Survey-weighted
mean positive reported minutes provide edge costs; shortest directed
travel-time paths determine which destinations are reachable within 45 or
60 minutes. Each destination's weight is its share of supported pooled
weighted arrivals for the purpose and scope. For each sex, the score is
averaged over the original survey-weighted residential distribution
conditional on starting outside the target set. The female disadvantage is
defined as male minus female reachable-arrival share. Its paired regional
contrast uses the same fixed time surfaces for women and men within each
scope. A 500-replicate shared Poisson household bootstrap reweights only
the residential distributions, conditional on the estimated edge costs
and targets; Table~\ref{tab:s12_time_home} gives unadjusted percentile
intervals. This is a conventional time-threshold \emph{proxy} based on
revealed OD edges and arrivals, not an inventory of opportunities or a
complete route-choice model. It lacks a sex-specific movement kernel by
construction and cannot be compared numerically to FPT steps.

A separate check identifies home-origin trips when a trip starts in its
household's residence UTAM. The full-population reference kernel,
connected network, destinations, and residential distributions stay fixed;
only the sex-specific empirical trip rows and their effective sample
sizes change. All trip records were assigned to households with a unique
residential UTAM. The pooled weighted share of home-origin trips was
0.623. Table~\ref{tab:s12_time_home} also reports the resulting FPT
point contrasts. They do not inherit intervals from the primary model.

\subsection{Paired bootstrap with reselected targets}
In 150 exploratory shared Poisson household replicates, we compared the
regional-minus-district FPT contrast under the fixed primary target sets
and under newly selected supported core-50 targets inside each replicate.
The original strongly connected components and population shrinkage
kernels were retained; sampled residential masses and sex-specific
transition row weights and effective sample sizes varied in both variants.
Table~\ref{tab:s13_reselected_targets} gives percentile intervals
from identical resamples, allowing a direct comparison of widths.
They are not simultaneous intervals, and the smaller run does not replace
the primary 500-replicate max-$t$ inference.

\section{Reproducibility and interpretation boundaries}
Tables S1--S11 summarize validated primary analyses and sensitivity checks;
Tables S12--S13 summarize the separate Gate 23 model comparisons and reduced
paired bootstrap. The corresponding analyses and inputs are documented in
the repository. The analysis is cross-sectional and descriptive. Survey
weights and clustered bootstrap inference propagate sampling uncertainty but do not
remove recall error, nonresponse bias, unobserved modal and temporal heterogeneity, or
the limited gender categories in the processed survey.
